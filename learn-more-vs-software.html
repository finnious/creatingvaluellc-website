Connect vs. Buy

The last thing you need is another tool to buy.

Creating Value LLC — the systems layer between the tools you already pay for and the people who keep your business running.

Book your free Systems Snapshot

The reflex is to buy. The reflex is usually wrong.

Something's broken in operations, so a vendor sells you a platform to fix it. Now you have a new subscription, a migration, another login for the team, and — critically — one more silo that doesn't talk to the tools you already run. The original gap (data in one system, the person who needs it in another) hasn't closed. You've just added a wall.

The uncomfortable truth: your systems already hold what you need. What to reorder, what's dead stock, what margins look like, what's overdue, who's still on a list that shouldn't be. It's trapped in exports and inboxes. The job isn't to buy a new source of data — it's to connect the sources you already pay for.

What we build on top of what you have

  • We turn raw exports into reports people use. A 31-column, 57,558-row nightly export becomes a purchasing report a buyer opens at 6 AM and acts on — no new database, no new app.
  • We reshape the same data per role. Buyers get cost and margin; the sales floor gets a customer-safe, price-only view; stockers get bin-sorted walk-lists. Six roles, one source, zero new tools.
  • We connect and route. A Google Groups structure you already had access to routed away ~750–800 unnecessary emails a week.
  • We make it efficient. One pipeline went from ~3,000 API calls to 3 — same output, faster and cheaper, on the same tools.

The ownership difference

When you buy software, your data lives in someone else's platform and your process bends to their features. When we build, everything runs on the tools you already own, shaped to your exact workflow — and you keep all of it. No per-seat fees compounding forever. No vendor to chase when it breaks. No lock-in.

Right for you if

You're on Google Workspace, established (roughly $5M–$50M, 5–100 people), with real people doing real work, and a stack of tools that don't connect because nobody's job is to connect them.
